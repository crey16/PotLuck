# PotLuck

PotLuck is a poker learning site for getting comfortable with the numbers behind good decisions. It combines short lessons, realistic practice hands, and quick drills so ideas like pot odds and expected value stop feeling like homework and start becoming instinct.

You do not need to be a math person, and you do not need to memorize everything before you begin. Start where you are, make a few decisions, and let the explanations do the rest.

## Getting started

Create an account and PotLuck will begin with a short nine-question placement assessment. It is not a test and it does not affect your stats. Its only job is to find a useful starting point in the course.

If you are unsure about an answer, choose **Not sure**. That is more helpful than guessing. You can also skip placement and return to it later from the Learn page.

Once you are in, your Home page shows the next lesson in your course, a recommended practice activity, the daily lesson, and your recent progress.

## A good first session

If you are not sure where to begin, this is a solid fifteen-minute routine:

1. Continue the next lesson on your Home page.
2. Try the daily decision.
3. Spend five minutes drilling the same idea.
4. Read the explanation after each answer, especially when you were right for the wrong reason.

Accuracy matters, but the real goal is to recognize the decision quickly and understand why it is right.

## Learn

The course is split into five modules that move from the foundations into practical decision-making and bankroll discipline. Lessons are short and mix explanation with checks for understanding. Nothing is permanently locked, so you can follow the course in order or revisit an earlier lesson whenever you want.

The Learn page also includes two practice labs:

- **Authored hands** give you a complete poker spot, ask you to commit to an action, and then explain the rule behind the answer.
- **Table decisions** make you reconstruct the important details—position, action, stack depth, and board—before choosing your line.

## Drill

The drill room is for repetition. Use **Mixed** when you want variety, or choose one topic when you want to work on a specific weakness:

- **Outs:** count the cards that complete your draw without counting dead outs.
- **Rule of 2 and 4:** estimate your chance of improving without reaching for a calculator.
- **Pot odds:** find the equity a call needs to break even.
- **Decision:** compare your price with your equity and choose whether to continue.
- **Implied odds:** work out how much more you need to win later to justify a call.
- **Expected value:** calculate the value of a call in dollars.
- **Bluffing:** practice break-even bluff frequency and minimum defense frequency.
- **Concepts:** recognize common strategic mistakes and discipline leaks.
- **Preflop:** practice standard six-max starting ranges.

Questions become less tidy as you improve. Early levels use clean numbers; later levels introduce more realistic and awkward sizing.

Need a formula while you work? Open **Reference** from the navigation, or press **R** from a drill.

## Daily lesson

The daily lesson is one focused decision that changes at midnight Eastern Time. It is a quick way to keep a streak alive and earn 15 XP when you do not have time for a longer session.

## Ranges and reference

The **Ranges** page is a visual guide to standard six-max preflop ranges for 100bb play and 2.5bb opens. Pick a scenario to see which hands usually raise, call, or fold.

Treat these as useful baseline ranges, not universal answers. Good preflop strategy changes with rake, stack depth, open size, and the people at your table.

The **Reference** page keeps the core poker-math formulas in one place. It is there to help while you are learning; needing to look something up is part of the process.

## Play and review

The **Play** area lets you apply what you have learned in complete hands instead of isolated questions. Afterward, open your recent-hand history to revisit decisions and look for patterns in your thinking.

## Progress, friends, and ranks

PotLuck tracks XP, levels, streaks, accuracy, activity, and eight underlying poker skills. Recommendations use that history to point you toward something worth practicing next.

You can add friends to compare profiles, stats, and streaks. The Ranks page shows the live XP leaderboard, while your profile controls what other players can see.

## One last thing

PotLuck is a study tool, not a promise that every table will behave like a textbook. Use it to build sound instincts, then stay curious about the players and circumstances in front of you. Poker still has luck in it—that is right there in the name.
